This is the page that surprises people, so it comes with the blunt version
first.

<Callout type="warn">
**Nothing reads your messages in the background.**

MCP tools only run when a client calls them. MyGist does not observe your
conversation. It cannot notice that you said something worth remembering unless
the client explicitly asks it to look.
</Callout>

So, by default:

- The assistant **reads** your persona when you ask about yourself.
- The assistant **writes** to it when you explicitly ask it to.
- Saying *"I've started learning Rust"* halfway through a chat about something
  else changes **nothing**.

That is deliberate. A tool that silently recorded everything you typed would be
a different product, and a harder one to trust with this kind of data.

## Turning suggestions on

If you want a conversation where things do get noticed, say so at the start:

> For this conversation, call `suggest_persona_update` on my messages and tell
> me when something is worth saving.

From then on the client passes what you say to `suggest_persona_update`, which
returns candidate updates. You still approve them. The tool proposes; it does
not write.

<Callout>
Worth putting in your client's system prompt or project instructions if you want
it always on. That is the closest thing to automatic capture that exists — and
it is still the client doing the work, not the server.
</Callout>

## How suggestions are judged

`suggest_persona_update` weighs what kind of statement it is looking at, because
not everything said is meant.

| Kind | Example | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Declarative | "I am a developer" | High confidence |
| Insight or reflection | "This helped me understand pricing" | High confidence, goes to the learning log |
| Hypothetical | "Maybe I should try Rust" | Low confidence |
| Venting | "TypeScript is killing me" | Usually ignored, unless there is an insight in it |
| Question | "Should I learn Rust?" | Ignored, unless it also states something |

Long messages are analysed sentence by sentence, so a paragraph of solid
statements ending in a question still yields the statements.

Confidence then decides what happens:

| Confidence | Result |
|---|---|
| 0.8 and above | Applied, and mentioned to you |
| 0.5 to 0.8 | You are asked first |
| Below 0.5 | Dropped silently |

### Insights go to the learning log

Conceptual and soft-skill learning counts, not just technical facts. "I realised
titles don't guarantee commitment" is a valid learning-log entry.

Those entries get a topic extracted from the surrounding conversation, a summary
of what led to the insight, and automatic tags — so the context of *why* you
learned something survives alongside the fact.

### Duplicates are checked first

Every suggestion is compared against what you already have, through the search
index. If it matches something, the suggestion is rewritten from an `add` into
an update targeting that entry.

Mentioning the same thing across five conversations does not leave you with five
copies of it.

## The honest limitation

Capture depends on the client actually calling the tool, and clients vary:

- Some do it readily when asked.
- Some forget after a handful of turns.
- None do it unprompted.

If accuracy matters more than convenience, write the entry yourself — see
[Writing](/use/writing) — or use [the editor](/use/editor). Treat capture as a
way to catch things you would otherwise lose, not as a reliable record.

## What to do at the end of a good conversation

The practical middle ground: when a conversation has produced something worth
keeping, ask for it directly.

> Anything from this conversation worth saving to my persona?

The client reviews the thread, proposes entries, and you approve them in one
pass. That gets you most of what automatic capture would, with none of the
guessing.
