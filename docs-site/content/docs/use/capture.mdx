<Callout type="warn">
**Nothing reads your messages in the background.**

MCP tools only run when a client calls them. MyGist does not observe your
conversation. It cannot notice that you said something worth remembering unless
the client explicitly asks it to look.
</Callout>

So, by default:

- The assistant **reads** your persona when you ask about yourself.
- The assistant **writes** to it when you explicitly ask it to.
- Anything it merely *infers* goes to a queue you review, and never straight
  into your persona.

That is a deliberate choice. A tool that silently recorded everything you typed
would be harder to trust with this kind of data.

## Two ways things get in

| You say | Tool | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| "Add Datadog to my skills" | `persona_modify` | Written immediately |
| *(mentions in passing that they now run the on-call dashboards)* | `propose_update` | Lands in your review queue |

The rule is one line: **explicit instruction writes, inference proposes.** No
confidence score decides on your behalf, and `propose_update` cannot write to
your persona at all.

## Turning proposals on

If you want a conversation where things get noticed, say so at the start:

> For this conversation, call `propose_update` when you notice something
> durable about me.

Worth putting in your client's system prompt or project instructions if you
want it always on. That is the closest thing to automatic capture that exists,
and the client is still the one doing the work.

## What a proposal carries

Every proposal must include two things, or the server rejects it:

- **`rationale`** is why the agent thinks this is durable, in its own words.
  This is what you read when deciding.
- **`evidence`** is your own words that prompted it. An agent that cannot quote
  you has inferred too far, and the requirement is what stops the queue filling
  with plausible guesses.

Each row shows both, under the change itself, and names the tool that
proposed it, such as "Claude Desktop", "Cursor" or "Codex", so you can tell
which of your clients is over-eager.

## Inbox and observations

Proposals come in two kinds, and they get their own tabs because they need
different amounts of thought.

### Inbox

Typed, schema-valid changes: a skill level that moved, a project that finished,
a person worth remembering. **Approve**, **edit then approve**, or **reject**.

An update says what it would replace, as "Notes: Paused for now → Weekly
tutor on Thursdays", so you see the value on record before you overwrite it. A
row that would change nothing says so.

Approve, Reject and Delete each show a confirmation that names the entry, such
as "Hana added to Circle", with **Undo**. Nothing is sent until it goes: a bar
along its foot shows the eight seconds running out. Confirmations stack, so
deciding the next row leaves the last one's Undo in place; hover the stack, or
Tab into it, to spread it out and stop every clock while you read. Leaving
Review sends anything still waiting. An approval's confirmation also links to
the section it went into.

Approving writes through exactly the same path `persona_modify` uses, so every
duplicate check and validation applies. Editing first is expected, because
agents get details slightly wrong often enough that correcting before approving
is normal: open the row and choose **Edit before approving**. A list, such as
tags, is edited as one comma-separated line. With suggestions turned on, a new
entry whose name is a whole sentence ("Plays five-a-side football every
Thursday with people from work") comes with an offer to tidy it: the name
("five-a-side football"), the sentence in its notes, and any field the
assistant left empty. **Tidy** applies it to the form; nothing is approved
until you approve.

To deal with several at once, tick their boxes, or **Select all**, and use the
bar that appears: **Approve**, **Reject**, or **Delete** on Observations.
Approving or rejecting several has one **Undo** for the lot, and the
confirmation counts what each did, as "Approved 4: 2 added, 2 updated". A row
you are editing is left out, so a bulk approve never drops your changes.

On a keyboard, press `j` to reach the first row, then `j` and `k` to move, `a`
to approve, `r` to reject, `e` to edit and `x` to select. `?` lists them all,
from anywhere on the page.

### Observations

Durable but ambiguous, where nothing in the schema fits. "Wants the
recommendation first, then the reasoning" is real, and there is no field for it.

Two actions: **promote** it into a typed entry wherever you think it belongs, or
**delete** it. Observations are a staging area. They feed no scope, so no agent
reads them until you have promoted them into real data.

Promoting asks for a section and a type, named as the editor names them ("When
I'm feeling..."), then shows that type's fields to fill in before it becomes
data. The section starts on the one the assistant suggested; the type starts
unchosen whenever there is more than one, so nothing is filed under a guess.
Under the type, a line says what it is for. Any type a section shows can take
an observation, including ones that need more than a name, such as a job:
**Promote** waits until the required fields are filled.

If your instance has suggestions turned on, a **Suggested** row offers the
three most likely places, from any section, one tap each, and the chosen
type's fields are filled in from what the observation says: "Doing an MA in
Digital Media at the University of Leeds" becomes Institution *University of
Leeds*, Degree *MA*, Field of study *Digital Media*, and the sentence goes to
the notes where the type has them. Every value is copied from the
observation's own words, a field it is unsure of stays empty, and when the
suggestion is sure the type is already chosen. Check the fields, change any of
them, and nothing is filed until you choose **Promote**.

If the same kind of observation keeps appearing and nothing fits, that is a
signal to add a section for it. See
[section packs](/run/section-packs).

### Stale

Review has a third tab that holds no suggestions at all. It lists your own
entries that have gone a long time without changing, from every section, so you
can **Keep** them or open them to update. See
[the editor](/use/editor#where-an-entry-came-from).

## Duplicates and rejections

Two behaviours keep the queue usable over months.

- **Rejected claims never come back.** Reject a proposal and it becomes a
  tombstone: no agent, in any client, can raise the same thing again. Without
  this, every tool re-suggests what you already declined, forever. The web
  app sends a rejection only once its confirmation has gone, so **Undo** on
  it puts the row back.
- **The same claim from three tools is one row.** Repeat proposals increment a
  counter instead of stacking up, so you see "seen 3×".

Observations are bounded too. Past 50 pending ones, the queue is trimmed back to
50, and the ones you have already opened go first, least-looked-at and oldest
before the rest. Observations you have never opened are left alone until the
queue passes 100, after which they are fair game as well. Clearing the tab
occasionally is the way to keep anything you care about.

## Limits

Capture depends on the client actually calling the tool, and clients vary:

- Some do it readily when asked.
- Some forget after a handful of turns.
- None do it unprompted.

If accuracy matters more than convenience, write the entry yourself. See
[Writing](/use/writing) or [the editor](/use/editor). Proposals are a way to
catch things you would otherwise lose, and they are not a complete record of a
conversation.

## At the end of a conversation

When a conversation has produced something worth keeping, ask for it directly:

> Anything from this conversation worth proposing to my persona?

The client reviews the thread, sends proposals, and you clear them in one pass
from the Review tab.
